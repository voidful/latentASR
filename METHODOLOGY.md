# Robust Latent Reasoning for Speech Recognition: Dynamic Gating and Trajectory Regularization

## 1. Abstract
Despite the remarkable success of foundation models in Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR), these models often struggle with out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, domain shifts, and complex linguistic contexts. We propose a novel Latent Reasoning architecture that augments a frozen ASR model (Qwen-Audio) with a lightweight, trainable "thought" generation module. Specifically, we inject Continuous Thoughts (Latent Deltas) into the LLM prefix before textual decoding. To ensure robustness across diverse domains, we introduce a **Soft Gated Injection** mechanism, **Trajectory Regularization**, and a **Value Head for Dynamic Halting**. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our approach not only improves state-of-the-art accuracy on in-domain data but also preserves strong generalization capabilities, overcoming the degradation issues commonly observed in prompt-tuning methods.

## 2. Methodology

Our architecture fundamentally relies on a frozen audio-language model. During inference or training, instead of directly decoding text from the encoded audio features, we insert $N$ Latent-Reasoning (`NT`) tokens into the context prefix. These tokens are dynamically updated through continuous reasoning steps before participating in the final autoregressive transcription.

### 2.1 Continuous Latent Reasoning (Continuous Thoughts)

The core reasoning process is formulated as an iterative refinement of latent states. Let $h_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times D}$ be the initial embeddings of the $K$ reasoning tokens, encoded alongside the audio context. The update rule for $N$ reasoning steps is defined as:

$$
h_t = h_{t-1} + \Delta_t, \quad t \in \{1, \ldots, N\}
$$

Where $\Delta_t$ is the proposed reasoning delta at step $t$, generated by passing the current prefix states through the frozen Large Language Model (LLM):

1. **Context Evolution:** We forward $h_{t-1}$ through the LLM, caching the `past_key_values` and evolving the `attention_mask` step-by-step. This directly aligns with the autoregressive nature of causal generation, ensuring train-infer consistency.
2. **Delta Generation (The "Semantic Translator"):** A fundamental difference between our approach and previous continuous thought methods like COCONUT (which directly feeds the deep hidden state $h_L$ back as input) is the prevention of semantic space mismatch and magnitude explosion. Directly looping $h_L$ back to the embedding layer corrupts the foundation model's learned expectations. Instead, we project the top-layer hidden state $c_t$ using a shared translation layer:
   $$ \tilde{\Delta}_t = \text{Norm}(\text{Proj}_{\Delta}(c_t)) \cdot \text{scale}_t $$
   This $\text{Proj}_{\Delta}$ acts as a "semantic translator," mapping the high-level LLM reasoning back into a safe, virtual token embedding space. By enforcing L2 normalization and a learnable $\text{scale}_t$ (matching the uniform magnitude of standard tokens), we strictly constrain the perturbation's physical volume, empowering the model with robust "Do-No-Harm" capabilities. Crucially, the same $\text{Proj}_{\Delta}$ is shared across all steps; step-awareness is achieved via step-specific temporal embeddings (`step_embed`), enabling distinct delta directions per iteration without parameter explosion.

### 2.2 Soft Gated Injection (Residual Protection)

A common point of failure for latent prompting is OOD degradation. When encountering unfamiliar acoustic features, the LLM may propose harmful $\Delta_t$ that corrupt the expected Gaussian/RMSNorm distribution space of the embeddings, leading to hallucination (e.g., repeating punctuation or producing out-of-vocabulary characters). 

To prevent this, we introduce a **Soft Gated Injection** layer $\mathcal{G}(\cdot)$, which acts as a residual protection mechanism. The gate predicts a confidence weight $\gamma_t \in (0, 1)$ based on both the base embedding and the proposed delta:

$$ \gamma_t = \sigma(W_{gate} [h_0 ; \tilde{\Delta}_t] + b_{gate}) $$
$$ h_t = h_{0} + (\gamma_t \odot \tilde{\Delta}_t) $$

Crucially, the gate weights $W_{gate}$ and $b_{gate}$ are initialized to $0$, ensuring $\gamma_t = 0.5$ at initialization (neutral start). Furthermore, we ensure the injected delta acts as strict additive noise without applying a destructive `LayerNorm` afterwards, fundamentally protecting the absolute magnitude space of the LLM's native embeddings.

## 3. Training Process and Regularization

To train the lightweight adapter (the projection heads, positional embeddings, and the Soft Gate) under the frozen backbone constraints, we design a multi-objective loss function consisting of three vital components:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{CE} + w_{cycle}(i) \cdot \mathcal{L}_{cycle} + w_{value} \cdot \mathcal{L}_{value}
$$

1.  **Cross Entropy ($\mathcal{L}_{CE}$):** The fundamental objective. It ensures that regardless of the internal latent trajectory, the final state yields the correct autoregressive transcription. It serves as the ultimate metric for correctness.
2.  **Trajectory Regularization ($\mathcal{L}_{cycle}$):** The safety tether. It constrains the distance between thought states and promotes temporal smoothness, preventing the reasoning delta from pushing the model into uncharted, catastrophic representation spaces.
3.  **Value Head Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{value}$):** The meta-cognitive evaluator. By predicting the expected Cross Entropy improvement (or token accuracy diff) brought by latent reasoning over the baseline, it teaches the model to self-assess and halt if further reasoning is deemed unhelpful.

### 3.1 Unrolled Truncated BPTT
To preserve causality and compute accurate gradients, the training forward pass utilizes strictly aligned unrolled computation paths. Instead of using a static initial prompt context, we evolve `past_key_values` and `attention_mask` incrementally through BPTT (Backpropagation Through Time). To avoid unstable gradients over $N$ steps, we detach the state $h_t$ locally before generating the next delta, essentially decomposing the process into 1-step truncated BPTT loops.

### 3.2 Trajectory Regularization ($\mathcal{L}_{cycle}$)
Latent vectors are prone to semantic "drift" over multiple steps. To bound the optimization space, we penalize the reasoning trajectory. Given the full trajectory $\mathcal{T} = [h_0, h_1, \ldots, h_N]$, we apply:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{cycle} = \alpha \underbrace{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{t=1}^N \|h_t - h_0\|^2}_{\text{Anchor Loss}} + (1 - \alpha) \underbrace{\frac{1}{N-1} \sum_{t=1}^{N-1} \|h_{t+1} - h_t\|^2}_{\text{Smoothness Loss}}
$$
This binds every intermediate state to the original acoustic identity ($h_0$) and enforces temporal smoothness.

### 3.3 Dynamic Halting via Value Head ($\mathcal{L}_{value}$)

To allow the architecture to skip unhelpful reasoning on simple/in-distribution queries, we attach a Value Head. During training, we compute a baseline forward pass (without reasoning) and a latent-augmented forward pass. The Value Head learns to predict the expected improvement via Mean Squared Error:

$$ v_{pred} = \tanh(W_v h_t) $$

The target is dynamically computed. By default, it uses the token-level accuracy difference. However, in extreme out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios (like cross-lingual generation where base accuracy is near zero), we introduce a crucial **CE-based Fallback**:
$$ target = \tanh \big( \text{Clamp}(\text{CE}_{baseline} - \text{CE}_{latent}, -2, 2) \times 0.5 \big) $$

This ensures that the Value Head always receives meaningful variance, recognizing when Latent Reasoning lowers the Cross Entropy even if token accuracy hasn't yet improved. During inference, if $v_{pred}$ drops below a predefined threshold, the cycle halts early (Dynamic Halting), saving compute and preventing hallucination.

### 3.4 Robustness Perturbations
During training, we apply **Latent Input Noise** ($\mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$) to $h_0$ and a **Latent Dropout** mechanism (e.g., 15% probability of omitting all reasoning tokens). These regularizations prevent the model from over-relying on the reasoning path and encourage resilient textual decoding.

## 4. Evaluation and Consistency

When evaluating ASR models, domain mismatches between the continuous features seen in training and inference severely impact performance. We strictly align training and inference evaluation logic:
1. **Mask Consistency:** We dynamically extract and supply the true `attention_mask` directly from the feature extractor, preventing padding noise from polluting the audio cross-attention layers.
2. **Deterministic Sequence Pad:** When Dynamic Halting activates at step $K < N$, we strictly zero-pad the remaining $\Delta$ updates up to $N$. Thus, the total token count of the sequence is strictly conserved dynamically, ensuring prompt stability before the language modeling head takes over.
